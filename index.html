§ 01–03
LAW 01

01 — An object keeps doing what it’s doing

“An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless a force acts on it.”

Perseverance does not simply begin moving on its own. With an inertial mass of 1,025 kilograms, the rover possesses inertia: a resistance to any change in its velocity. While parked on the floor of Jezero Crater, gravitational pull downward is exactly counteracted by the normal force of the ground upward. The net force is zero.

To begin rolling, electric motors inside the rover’s wheel hubs must supply torque, creating an unbalanced horizontal force that overcomes static terrain friction and rolling resistance.

Once the rover is in motion, Newton’s First Law also dictates what happens when the motors shut down. Unlike an interplanetary spacecraft that coasts continuously through the vacuum of space without fuel, a rover on Mars experiences continuous external contact forces: loose regolith displacement, wheel resistance, and uneven rock inclines. These external opposing forces continuously act on the rover, decelerating it back to rest unless motor torque continues to balance them.

KEY PRINCIPLE · INERTIA

Inertia depends only on mass, not gravity. Even though Perseverance weighs ~62% less on Mars than on Earth, its horizontal reluctance to start or stop moving is identical on both planets.

§ 02
LAW 02

02 — More force means more acceleration

F = ma or rearranged: a = F / m

Newton’s Second Law connects three fundamental physical quantities: net applied force (F), inertial mass (m), and the resulting acceleration (a). If you apply a greater net force, the acceleration increases proportionally. If mass is greater, acceleration decreases for that same applied force.

When Perseverance’s electric drive motors apply force through the wheel rims to the ground, the entire 1,025 kg vehicle accelerates. Because the rover has a substantial mass, altering its velocity—whether nudging it forward from a dead stop or climbing out of a sandy depression—demands deliberate, calibrated force.

CRITICAL PHYSICS NOTE · GRAVITY VS. INERTIA

Mars surface gravity is roughly 38% of Earth’s (~3.72 m/s²), which reduces the rover’s weight from ~10,055 N on Earth to ~3,813 N on Mars. However, lower gravity does not make the rover accelerate faster horizontally. Horizontal inertia depends solely on mass (m = 1,025 kg). A 300 N motor push produces an identical 0.293 m/s² acceleration on Mars as it would in flat vacuum on Earth.

In fact, lower gravity makes driving harder: the normal force is smaller, which reduces the maximum traction before wheel slippage occurs!

§ 03
LAW 03

03 — Every push has a push back

“For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.”

Forces in nature never exist in isolation; they always occur in matched interaction pairs. When one object exerts a force on a second object, the second object simultaneously exerts an equal-magnitude force back on the first, pointing in the direct opposite direction.

A common misconception is that a rover “pushes air” or “simply pushes itself forward.” Mars’ atmosphere is less than 1% as dense as Earth’s, offering almost zero aerodynamic resistance or propulsion medium.

The true motion mechanism is strictly the wheel-ground interface:

  • 1
    Action: The rover’s rotating wheel cleats push backward against the Martian regolith and bedrock (Fwheel on ground).
  • 2
    Reaction: The Martian ground pushes forward against the wheel with an equal and opposite force (Fground on wheel).

It is this reaction force from the Martian ground that actually propels the 1,025 kg rover forward. If Perseverance encounters extremely loose, powdery sand with poor shear strength, the ground shears before it can supply the forward reaction force, resulting in wheel slip.

ACTION-REACTION PAIR · CONTACT PATCH CLOSE-UP CROSS-SECTION VIEW
The Interaction Pair: The action force (Fwheel→ground) and reaction force (Fground→wheel) are equal in magnitude (|F₁| = |F₂|) and opposite in direction (F₁ = -F₂). Crucially: they act on different bodies, which is why they do not cancel each other out and motion occurs.

PHYSICAL SUMMARY

The Laws at a Glance

I FIRST LAW

Motion changes when an external force acts.

Perseverance remains stationary on the floor of Jezero Crater until its wheel actuators generate enough torque to overcome ground resistance, and will slowly coast to a stop once driving torque ceases.

II SECOND LAW

Force, mass, and acceleration are connected.

Because Perseverance has an inertial mass of 1,025 kg, accelerating the vehicle requires calibrated motor force via F = ma, regardless of Mars’ lower gravitational pull.

III THIRD LAW

Forces always come in interacting pairs.

The rover advances across the Martian surface solely because the bedrock and regolith push forward against the wheels in direct reaction to the wheels pushing backward.

ENGINEERING AT 225 MILLION KILOMETRES

Why Newtonian Mechanics Matter to JPL Engineers

Autonomous Navigation (AutoNav)

Radio signals between Earth and Mars take anywhere from 5 to 20 minutes each way. Mission controllers cannot steer in real time with a joystick. Perseverance’s onboard navigation computers continuously calculate the rover’s inertia, momentum, and braking distance using Newton’s Second Law to safely skirt boulders without human intervention.

Wheel Slip & Hazard Avoidance

On steep 30° crater rim slopes, the normal force drops (N = mg cos θ), drastically reducing available grip under Newton’s Third Law. Flight software measures the difference between commanded wheel revolutions and visual odometer tracking to halt motors before wheels excavate themselves into loose regolith.

Precision Touchdown (EDL)

During the famous Sky Crane maneuver, thrusters fired upward to precisely match the downward pull of Martian gravity, creating zero net force (ΣF = 0) so the 1,025 kg rover could be lowered onto its wheels at a gentle, constant 0.75 m/s.
